## 0. The result that reframes everything

### The futures term structure identifies only 3 of 5 risk-neutral parameters

Write the closed form in basis-function form:

$$A(\tau) = c_1\tau + c_2\left(1-e^{-2\kappa\tau}\right) + c_3\left(1-e^{-\kappa\tau}\right)$$

and define the composite

$$\boxed{\;m \;=\; \alpha^{\mathbb Q} + \frac{\sigma_1\sigma_2\rho}{\kappa}\;}$$

Then, with $P = \sigma_1\sigma_2\rho$:

$$c_1 = r - m + \tfrac12\frac{\sigma_2^2}{\kappa^2}, \qquad c_2 = \frac{\sigma_2^2}{4\kappa^3}, \qquad c_3 = \frac{m}{\kappa} - \frac{\sigma_2^2}{\kappa^3}$$

- All three coefficients — hence $A(\tau)$ entirely — depend only on $(\kappa,\sigma_2,m)$. $B(\tau)$ depends only on $\kappa$.
- **$\sigma_1$, $\rho$ and $\alpha^{\mathbb Q}$ are individually unidentifiable from futures prices** at any number of maturities, on any number of dates, at zero noise.
- Stronger than the familiar "$\sigma_1$ and $\rho$ enter only as a product": even $P$ is not identified, being confounded with $\alpha^{\mathbb Q}$ inside $m$.

**Verified three ways** (float64):
- Jacobian SVD, 12-maturity synthetic grid: `[1.52e-1, 6.93e-3, 2.49e-5, 1.02e-17, 7.42e-18]` — **rank 3, nullity 2**. Real WTI 8-slot grid: same structure.
- Invariance: holding $(\kappa,\sigma_2,m)$ fixed while sweeping $\sigma_1\in[0.16,1.24]$, $\rho\in[-0.94,0.83]$, $\alpha^{\mathbb Q}\in[-0.11,0.49]$ changes $A(\tau)$ by $\le 5.6\times10^{-17}$.
- Profile likelihood (`figures/identifiability/ident1_profile_likelihood.pdf`): $\kappa$ and $m$ curve steeply, $\sigma_2$ curves 2–3 orders of magnitude more weakly, $\sigma_1$ and $\rho$ are exactly flat.

### Why this is a contribution, not a failure

| | $\kappa$ | $\sigma_2$ | $\sigma_1$ | $\rho$ | $\alpha^{\mathbb Q}$ | $P$ | **$m$** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| TRUE | 1.876 | 0.527 | 0.393 | 0.766 | 0.0779 | 0.1586 | **0.16248** |
| AP-PEN | 1.874 | 0.518 | 0.497 | 0.818 | 0.0482 | 0.2108 | **0.16065** |
| CV over 20 draws | 1.3% | 9.0% | 49.4% | 51.1% | 265% | 70.6% | **3.8%** |

The estimator recovers everything the data contains to within ~4%, and fails
only on quantities **no futures-only estimator could recover**. Report
$(\kappa,\sigma_2,m)$ as the estimand.

⚠️ This **supersedes** `project_summary.md` §3.7 (which states nullity 1) and
§4.1 (which claims fixing $\lambda_2$ resolved the problem — see §1.4).

**In the thesis:** `main(3).tex` §3.3 `sec:identifiability`, Fig. `fig:identifiability`.

## 1. Stage 1 — Synthetic validation

**Setup:** 100 ℙ-paths, 1000 Euler–Maruyama steps, $T=10$yr, 12 maturities,
log-price noise $\sigma_\epsilon=0.01$. $\psi$ init perturbed 30–50% off truth;
$\lambda_2$ fixed **+30% off** (deliberately not an oracle). 40k epochs.

### 1.1 What this stage IS for
- The only stage with a ground-truth $\delta_t$, so the only place accuracy can be *measured*.
- Characterising the identifiability boundary with controlled noise.

### 1.2 What it is NOT for
- **Not evidence the method works on real data.** The panel is generated by exactly the model being fitted: zero misspecification by construction.
- **Not a test of "the PINN idea"** — with $A$ and $B$ analytic there is no PDE residual (§5).
- Not a generalisation test: it scores in-sample fit on the training dates.

### 1.3 The headline comparison — and the control that changes it

Five seeds, each a full refit:

| Method | δ RMSE | corr | increment std vs truth | training |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| No-skill (constant) | 0.2455 | — | 0% | — |
| MLP | 0.05093 ± 0.00367 | 0.9781 ± 0.0032 | 32.1 ± 2.7% | 40k epochs |
| AP-PEN | 0.05295 ± 0.00196 | 0.9764 ± 0.0018 | 31.7 ± 2.0% | 40k epochs |
| AP-PEN (ARB) | 0.06552 ± 0.00330 | 0.9705 ± 0.0052 | 24.2 ± 2.0% | 40k epochs |
| **Closed-form LS** | **0.00873** | **+0.9994** | **102.8%** | **none** |

- Against the no-skill baseline the network looks strong (~79% RMSE reduction).
- Against the **closed-form least-squares inversion it is 5.8× worse**, and LS reproduces the true innovation structure essentially perfectly (102.8%) where the network retains under a third of it.
- LS also recovers the identified parameters better: $\kappa$ 1.888 (true 1.876), $\sigma_2$ 0.521 (0.527), $m$ 0.1607 (0.1625).
- ⚠️ **A third single-seed reversal.** On seed 42 AP-PEN beat MLP by 13% (0.0499 vs 0.0571) — the number previously reported. Over five seeds **MLP is better by 3.8%**, a difference of **0.65 pooled standard deviations**: statistically indistinguishable. Together with §2.2 and §4.2 this is now a consistent pattern, and it has a single explanation (§4.4) — `e_sde` is stop-gradiented from the path net, so it has no mechanism by which to improve path recovery at all.

**This is the single most important control in the thesis** and it does not
favour the neural parameterisation on synthetic data. See
`figures/identifiability/ls1_delta_recovery.pdf` — the LS path sits on top of
the truth while the network traces a smooth low-frequency approximation.

⚠️ `project_summary.md`'s table (MLP 0.384 / PINN 0.049 / …) is the
**dual-network** notebook at a different epoch count and does not describe the
current model.

### 1.4 The identifiability "fix" traded bias for variance

| $P = \sigma_1\sigma_2\rho$ (true 0.1586) | mean | std | CV | character |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dual-network | 0.0648 | 0.0092 | 14% | **stuck at init** — large bias, small variance |
| Single-network + fixed $\lambda_2$ | 0.2424 | 0.1712 | **71%** | **wanders** — small bias, huge variance |
| $m$, either architecture | ≈0.164 | ≈0.007 | ≈4% | identified in both |

Fixing $\lambda_2$ de-biased the estimator without making the direction
identifiable. A ±1σ interval for $\rho$ is $[0.34,\,1.06]$ — wider than $\rho$'s
own admissible range. **Bias was converted into variance, not eliminated.**

### 1.5 The network oversmooths — three independent diagnostics

- **Increment std:** true 0.0533; recovered **24–32%** of it across 5 seeds (MLP 32.1±2.7%, AP-PEN 31.7±2.0%, ARB 24.2±2.0%). LS: **102.8%**.
- **`e_sde` retention:** deterministic floor −2.0336, true path −1.5177. MLP retains **2.0%**, AP-PEN **8.0%**, ARB **−40.7%** (smoother than a deterministic path).
- **MSE decomposition:** **≥96% of error is the correlation-shortfall term**. Level bias ≈ 0, amplitude ratio 0.97.

The marginal std is nearly right; the *increments* are 3–5× too small. Causes,
in likely order: spectral bias of a tanh MLP in $t$; $B(\tau)$ attenuation
($|B|\le1/\kappa\approx0.53$, $\to0$ as $\tau\to0$); daily SNR only ~2.7.

**Consequence:** the model recovers the *level* well and the *dynamics* poorly.
Anything downstream needing $\delta$'s volatility would be badly served. That
the LS baseline does not share this defect localises the cause squarely in the
neural parameterisation.

### 1.6 Noise sensitivity

| | δ RMSE | $\kappa$ CV | $\sigma_2$ CV | $m$ CV |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $\sigma_\epsilon=0.01$ | 0.0504 ± 0.0030 | 1.3% | 9.0% | 3.8% |
| $\sigma_\epsilon=0.10$ | 0.0625 ± 0.0032 | 9.2% | 57.7% | 30.8% |

Path recovery is robust (+24% for 10× noise — the cross-sectional average over
12 maturities does the denoising); parameter recovery degrades 6–8× faster.
$\sigma_2$ degrades furthest, consistent with being the most weakly identified
of the three estimable quantities.

**In the thesis:** `tab:synth_psi_multidraw`, `tab:noise_sensitivity`.

### 1.7 Limitations of Stage 1
- **Zero model misspecification** — the DGP *is* the estimating equation. The biggest caveat on every synthetic number.
- Euler–Maruyama simulation of an OU process whose **exact** transition is available; `e_sde` uses the same approximation, so the bias partly cancels and is undetectable from within.
- $\tau$ fixed per slot; contracts never mature. $r$ constant at 0.05.
- **float32** — residual/hinge readings below ~1e-5 are at the precision floor. The reported $e_{\delta\text{-floor}} = 1.4\times10^{-10}$ is below that floor and should not be quoted as a meaningful zero.

## 2. Stage 2 — Real WTI application

**Setup:** 2770 dates (2015-01-02 → 2026-02-19), 8 maturity slots, per-date FRED
$r_t$. $\psi$ initialised at the Kalman filter's fit, $\lambda_2$ fixed at the
KF's $\hat\lambda=0.439$. **Now run over 5 seeds.**

### 2.1 What this stage IS / IS NOT for
- **IS:** the actual thesis object — inverting for $\delta_t$ where it is genuinely latent; testing whether the constraints do useful work outside the simulator.
- **IS NOT:** a measurement of accuracy. No ground truth exists. Every number here is agreement with another model, curve-fit quality, or parameter plausibility.
- **IS NOT** a validation of the GS model for WTI. You assumed it and inverted it.

### 2.2 Results, 5 seeds

| variant | corr vs KF | RMSE vs KF | $\kappa$ | $m$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kalman (benchmark) | — | — | 0.426 | 0.081 |
| **NULL: constant at KF mean** | — | **0.1078** | — | — |
| Closed-form LS (exact MLE) | 0.922 | 0.112 | 0.951 | 0.187 |
| MLP | 0.9176 ± 0.0012 | 0.1089 ± 0.0004 | 0.928 ± 0.004 | 0.188 ± 0.001 |
| AP-PEN | 0.9170 ± 0.0010 | 0.1089 ± 0.0004 | 0.927 ± 0.003 | 0.189 ± 0.001 |
| AP-PEN (ARB) | 0.9469 ± 0.0034 | 0.0348 ± 0.0010 | **0.100 ± 0.000** | −0.030 ± 0.012 |

Three things follow, and the seed spreads are small enough to make each of them
definitive rather than suggestive:

1. **MLP and AP-PEN are statistically indistinguishable.** Correlations differ by 0.0006 against a seed std of 0.001; RMSEs agree to four decimals. Exactly what the architecture predicts — they differ only through the level of $\alpha^{\mathbb Q}$ (§4.3).
2. **Both are stably worse than a constant** (0.1089 ± 0.0004 vs 0.1078). Not an unlucky draw. On this metric they have **negative skill**.
3. **AP-PEN (ARB) hits the $\kappa$ floor on every seed** (0.100 ± 0.000) and its $\hat\delta$ minimum is exactly −0.300, the $\delta_{\min}$ guardrail, on every seed. It is not finding a plausible path; it is being **clamped to the boundary of the admissible region**.

### 2.3 The Kalman filter is the outlier, not the network

Pairwise RMSE between recovered $\hat\delta_t$ series
(`figures/identifiability/ls2_agreement_matrix.pdf`):

| | Kalman | MLP | AP-PEN | ARB | LS |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Kalman** | — | 0.109 | 0.109 | 0.040 | 0.112 |
| **MLP** | 0.109 | — | 0.014 | 0.121 | 0.033 |
| **AP-PEN** | 0.109 | 0.014 | — | 0.122 | 0.032 |
| **LS** | 0.112 | 0.033 | 0.032 | 0.128 | — |

- The three cross-sectional estimators agree with each other to **0.014–0.033**; each differs from the filter by **≈0.11**.
- **The exact MLE also "fails" the vs-KF metric** (0.112, worse than every neural variant). A metric that penalises the maximum-likelihood solution of the model is not measuring estimator quality — it is measuring *cross-section versus state space*.
- ARB agrees with the KF (0.040) while disagreeing with LS (0.128) — it is not finding a better answer, it has been squashed into a KF-like band by its hinges.

**Reframe the whole comparison** from "is AP-PEN accurate?" to "what does a
cross-sectional inversion imply that a state-space filter does not?"

### 2.4 The April 2020 excursion is not an artefact

- MLP/AP-PEN reach **−1.26 to −1.28**; the KF stays at −0.29.
- **The exact MLE reaches −1.65**, and its six lowest values are all April 2020.
- So the excursion is **what Gibson–Schwartz implies from the observed curve**, not something neural training invents. The KF's narrow range comes from its state-transition prior suppressing it.

**Consequence for the constraint argument:** the hinge is **injecting prior
economic information the data does not contain**, not repairing an estimation
failure. That is a legitimate modelling choice, but it must be framed that way
— "the constraint finds the right answer" is not supportable.

### 2.5 The unfloored collapse, and why the floor does not cure it

Without the $\kappa$ floor, AP-PEN (ARB) collapses to $\kappa=0.0028$,
$\sigma_1=0.0051$, $\alpha^{\mathbb Q}=-4.46$, $\alpha^{\mathbb P}=+4.37$.
Mechanism, fully traceable:

1. Hinges push the path net toward a flat, narrow $\hat\delta$ (std 0.088 vs 0.182).
2. Prices must still be fitted, so $A(\tau)$ must compensate.
3. **`step2a` trains the Q-group on $e_{data}$ alone, and $\alpha^{\mathbb Q}$ is derived, not free.** The only levers on its level are $\sigma_2$ and $\kappa$.
4. $\alpha^{\mathbb Q} = \alpha^{\mathbb P} - \sigma_2\lambda_2/\kappa$ has a **$1/\kappa$ pole**: at $\kappa=0.0028$, $\sigma_2\lambda_2/\kappa = 8.83$.
5. Once $\kappa\approx0$, $\alpha^{\mathbb P}$ **drops out of `e_sde`** (the OU conditional mean → $\delta_i$), so it drifts unopposed.
6. $B(\tau)\to-\tau$: the two-factor model has degenerated into a **deterministic cost-of-carry curve** (verified: $B(1.875)=-1.8701$ vs $-\tau=-1.875$).

**The §4.1 fix and this failure are the same line of code.** With
$\kappa_{\min}=0.1$ imposed, the variant converges *exactly onto the floor* on
all five seeds, with $\sigma_1$ still collapsing to 0.003. **The floor bounds
the pathology without curing it** — the hinge penalties, not the
parameterisation or the seed, drive the degeneracy.

⚠️ `project_summary.md` §4.5 reports ARB as the *best* real-data variant with
$\kappa=0.54$, $\rho=0.90$. That describes a run that no longer exists.

**In the thesis:** `tab:real_kalman_comparison`, `tab:real_psi_pathology`.

### 2.6 Do the hinge constraints work? — the metric that was never reported

Your evaluation scheme promises a **constraint activity rate** as a headline
metric. It appears nowhere in the thesis, and it is the most direct evidence
for the DC-PINN inequality mechanism you are extending. Computed
(`scripts/experiments/constraint_activity.py`):

| Panel | Series | cac violated | rcac violated | δ̂ < δ_min |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Real WTI** | Observed panel (model-free) | 1.84% | 0.00% | — |
| | MLP (no hinges) | 1.98% | 0.00% | 7.44% |
| | AP-PEN (no hinges) | 2.00% | 0.00% | 6.90% |
| | **AP-PEN (ARB), hinges on** | **0.00%** | 0.00% | **0.69%** |
| **Synthetic** | Observed panel (model-free) | 0.70% | 0.03% | **4.30%** |
| | MLP (no hinges) | 0.36% | 0.00% | 3.30% |
| | AP-PEN (no hinges) | 0.32% | 0.00% | 3.50% |
| | AP-PEN (ARB), hinges on | 0.00% | 0.00% | 0.20% |

**Yes, they work — on real data, decisively.** The observed WTI panel violates
the cash-and-carry ceiling on 1.84% of points, on 135 dates concentrated
entirely in **2015 (42), 2016 (55) and 2020 (38)** — the shale-glut contango
and the storage-exhaustion episode. The unconstrained variants inherit and
slightly amplify it (1.98–2.00%), which is correct behaviour for an estimator
fitting the data faithfully. ARB drives it to **exactly zero** and cuts
implausible convenience-yield states by an order of magnitude (7.4% → 0.7%).

**And the synthetic/real contradiction is now fully resolved and quantified.**
The *true* δ_t of the data-generating process lies below δ_min on **4.30%** of
dates. The unconstrained variants track that faithfully (3.3–3.5%); ARB
suppresses it to 0.20%. On synthetic data the hinge is not correcting an error
— **it is penalising states the process genuinely visits.** That is exactly why
it degrades recovery there and improves it on real data, and it is now a
measured fact rather than an inference.

**The transferable rule:** whether the constraint earns its cost is decided by
the *observed* violation rate, which is computable from
$(F^{\text{obs}}, S_t, \tau, r_t)$ **before any model is fitted**. That is a
genuinely useful result for anyone adopting the DC-PINN mechanism.

**Two honest qualifications.** (i) The **rcac floor never binds anywhere** —
0.00% in both panels. $\delta_{\max}=0.75$ is inert, so of the three hinges
only two do any work, and the thesis should say so rather than let the reader
assume all three contribute. (ii) ARB pays for this in three measured ways: a
3× in-sample price penalty (§4.2), a degenerate ψ the κ floor bounds but does
not cure (§2.5), and worse latent-state recovery wherever the bounds are not
the binding economics (§1.3, §4.3).

**In the thesis:** `sec:constraint_activity`, `tab:constraint_activity` — newly
added, since none of this was previously reported.

### 2.7 Limitations of Stage 2
- **Circularity:** $\psi$ is initialised *at* the KF's estimates and $\lambda_2$ is *fixed* at the KF's $\hat\lambda$. Agreement is partly assumed. An independently-initialised run is still outstanding.
- $\lambda_2$ fixed at a value estimated on the same data being evaluated — a mild in-sample leak.
- $r$ is per-date but $\tau$ is not: contracts do not roll down. Material for front contracts over an 11-year sample.
- $u=0.30$ and $\delta_{\max}=0.75$ are literature-consistent but were also cross-checked against violation rates *on the evaluation panel* — disclosed in the tex, but a genuine lookahead caveat.
- 2020-04-20 is dropped, but the surrounding week is not, so the model fits the most distressed prices in the sample with the single most informative day missing.
- **No uncertainty quantification** on $\hat\delta$. The KF gives a filtered variance for free.

## 3. Stage 3 — Kalman filter benchmark

**Setup:** joint latent $(x_t=\ln S_t, \delta_t)$, EM ℙ-transition, exact affine
ℚ-measurement (a plain KF is exact), $\lambda$ estimated so the measure-change
relation is a constraint. MLE via prediction-error decomposition. **x64 enabled.**

### 3.1 What it IS / IS NOT for
- **IS:** the only independent, non-PINN estimate from the same data; the literature-standard method (Schwartz 1997; Krul 2008).
- **IS NOT ground truth.** It is another model's opinion with its own misspecification. Say "agreement", never "accuracy".

### 3.2 Results
$\hat\kappa=0.426$, $\hat\sigma_1=0.440$, $\hat\sigma_2=0.173$, $\hat\rho=0.888$,
$\hat\alpha^{\mathbb Q}=-0.077$, $\hat\lambda=0.439$; log-lik 84707.
$\hat\delta_t$ mean +0.0395, std 0.1078, range [−0.293, +0.232] — plausible
throughout, **including April 2020**, without any constraint forcing it.
In-sample innovation RMSE 0.0263; futures-only inferred spot tracks cash to 6.88%.

### 3.3 The comparison is not apples-to-apples, and it favours the PINN
- The KF's $\hat\delta$ is **filtered** (causal); its innovation RMSE is a genuine one-step-ahead out-of-sample error.
- AP-PEN's $\hat\delta$ is effectively **smoothed** — the network sees all 11 years at once.
- The correct comparisons are AP-PEN vs the **RTS smoother**, or both on held-out dates. The second is now run (§4.2): on the 2024 holdout, with matched information sets, the filter degrades by 1.07× against the neural variants' 5.7–6.7×. The RTS smoother is declared future work in the methodology and would refine the in-sample comparison; the holdout result makes it the less urgent of the two.

### 3.4 Why the KF can identify $\sigma_1$ and $\rho$ when AP-PEN cannot
- The KF's **measurement** equation has the same rank-3 limitation (§0) — same pricing relation.
- Its **transition** equation adds information: $\sigma_1$ enters the conditional variance of $\Delta x_t$, $\rho$ the cross-covariance of state innovations.
- **AP-PEN routes all $\psi$ learning through `step2a`, a purely cross-sectional objective**, so it structurally cannot identify either — at any training length.

**This is architectural, not a tuning failure.** For *parameter estimation* the
KF is strictly better posed, because it uses an information channel AP-PEN
discards. The PINN's competitive claim must rest on the **path**, not $\psi$.

### 3.5 Limitations (the notebook's own list, plus)
- $\kappa$–$\lambda$ entanglement; don't over-interpret single parameters.
- EM rather than the **exact** OU transition, which is available in closed form.
- No floor/ceiling on $\hat\delta$ (Krul moves to CIR in his Ch. 5).
- Fixed mean $\tau$ per slot — same simplification as the PINN.
- **Single MLE start.** "Converged" = L-BFGS-B stopped. Krul runs multi-start. Given §0's flat directions this is a real risk.
- 8 free measurement-noise parameters can absorb misfit that belongs to the model.
- No parameter standard errors (the MLE Hessian gives them nearly free — though note §0: for $\sigma_1$/$\rho$ from the measurement channel alone, there is no curvature to compute them from).

## 4. Stage 4 — Temporal holdout

**Setup:** train `< 2024-01-01` (2237 dates), test `≥ 2024-01-01` (533, 19.2%).
Metric = log-price RMSE. **Now run over 5 seeds.**

### 4.1 What it IS / IS NOT for
- **IS:** the only genuinely out-of-sample test; a direct stress-test of the fact that $\hat\delta_\phi$ is a plain function of normalised time, so every test date is neural **extrapolation**.
- **IS NOT** a Kalman comparison — the KF is not refit on this split. On real data it also cannot test $\delta$ recovery directly (nothing to score against); §4.3 runs the same split on synthetic data, where it can.

### 4.2 The single-seed result does not replicate

| variant | in-sample | out-of-sample | ratio | per-seed range |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Kalman, forecast only (like-for-like)** | 0.02802 | 0.03007 | **1.07×** | (deterministic) |
| Kalman, filtered through test window | 0.02802 | 0.01638 | 0.58× | (deterministic) |
| LS, $\delta$ re-solved per test date | 0.01424 | 0.00984 | 0.69× | (deterministic) |
| LS, $\delta$ frozen at last trained value | 0.01424 | 0.03869 | 2.72× | (deterministic) |
| MLP | 0.02486 ± 0.00118 | 0.14581 ± 0.17861 | **5.73× ± 6.81** | [1.50, 19.24] |
| AP-PEN | 0.02429 ± 0.00129 | 0.16609 ± 0.12409 | **6.71× ± 4.83** | [1.80, 14.70] |
| AP-PEN (ARB) | 0.07181 ± 0.03624 | 0.05517 ± 0.00691 | **0.93× ± 0.33** | [0.42, 1.26] |

- **On seed 42 alone**: MLP 8.86×, AP-PEN 1.69× — which reads as strong evidence that the physics term buys generalisation.
- **Over five seeds the ordering reverses in the mean** and the ranges overlap almost entirely. **No such advantage is demonstrable.** The apparent effect was initialisation variance.
- Only AP-PEN (ARB) generalises dependably (0.93× ± 0.33), because its hinges bound the path so extrapolation cannot run away. It pays ~3× the in-sample price error for that.
- The LS decomposition brackets the achievable: 0.69× if only $\psi$ must generalise, 2.72× for a non-extrapolating path.
- **The incumbent baseline is now run** (`scripts/experiments/kf_holdout.py`). The KF, refit on the training window and given *exactly the neural models' information set* — state propagated forward from the cutoff, no test observations, observed spot — degrades by **1.07×**. Its filtered variant (allowed to keep updating on test data) reaches 0.58×, and the gap between the two measures how much of its advantage comes from that privilege.
- KF ψ̂ refit on `<2024` only: κ 0.4376, σ₁ 0.4728, σ₂ 0.1888, ρ 0.8882, λ 0.3111, α^P 0.0441, α^Q −0.0901; converged, nll −67566.9, in-sample innovation RMSE 0.02802. Close to the full-sample fit (κ 0.426, ρ 0.888), so the refit is stable rather than a different model.
- **The unconstrained neural variants are 5–7× worse than the incumbent on the same task.** Only AP-PEN (ARB) is competitive (0.93×) — but by being *bounded*, not by forecasting. The KF's δ̂ decays 0.146 → 0.084 over the test window, reverting toward its fitted $\alpha^{\mathbb P}=0.044$: a real forecast carrying information. ARB merely fails to move. §4.3 makes that distinction explicit by scoring the state itself.

### 4.3 The synthetic holdout — where δ *can* be scored — is worse still

The same split on the synthetic panel, where $\delta_t$ is known over the
held-out window, so out-of-sample **state** error is measured rather than
proxied by price fit. 5 seeds, train $t\le8$yr, test final 2 years:

| variant | δ-RMSE in | δ-RMSE out | **vs no-skill** | corr out |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **No-skill (constant)** | — | **0.20788** | **1.00×** | — |
| MLP | 0.04737 ± 0.00131 | 0.41137 ± 0.14761 | **1.98×** | +0.564 ± 0.314 |
| AP-PEN | 0.04868 ± 0.00188 | 0.29669 ± 0.15342 | **1.43×** | +0.718 ± 0.072 |
| AP-PEN (ARB) | 0.06073 ± 0.00289 | 0.52459 ± 0.15618 | **2.52×** | −0.410 ± 0.592 |

**Every variant is worse than predicting a constant out-of-sample.** In-sample
each recovers δ to ~0.05, a fifth of the no-skill level; two years past the
cutoff all three are 1.4–2.5× *worse* than a constant, a degradation of 6–9×.

Worse, the out-of-sample correlations are **unstable in sign**: MLP ranges
−0.05 to +0.80, ARB −0.81 to +0.77. An estimator that may correlate positively
or negatively with the truth depending on initialisation carries no usable
information about the held-out window.

**This is the cleanest statement of the architecture's central limitation**, and
it follows from the design, not from tuning. $\hat\delta_\phi$ is a function of
normalised time alone; past the cutoff a tanh network saturates to a constant
unrelated to the true path, and nothing in the objective supplies the dynamics
that would prevent it — `e_sde`, the only channel encoding the ℙ-dynamics, is
stop-gradiented away from the path net (§4.4). **The model interpolates the
latent state well and does not extrapolate it at all.**

Note also that **ARB's protective behaviour does not reappear here** — it is
the *worst* variant on this split. Same explanation as §1: on the synthetic
panel the guardrails sit outside the realised range of $\delta_t$ and never
bind, so they cannot bound the extrapolation the way they do on real prices.
The stability in §4.2 is a property of the constraints *binding*, not a general
property of the constrained variant.

### 4.4 The previously stated mechanism is wrong

`project_summary.md` §4.6 explains the gap as *"the OU-consistency prior gives
the path something to revert toward outside its trained domain"*. **The code
does not do this:**

```python
delta_hat_sde = lax.stop_gradient(delta_hat)
e_sde = vmap(sde_nll)(delta_hat_sde[:-1], delta_hat_sde[1:], dt)
```

- `e_sde`'s gradient w.r.t. the path net is **identically zero**, in-domain and out. It exerts **no force on $\hat\delta_\phi$ anywhere**.
- `step2b` optimises it w.r.t. $\alpha^{\mathbb P}$ **only**.
- The notebook confirms the consequence: `APPINN_nobal` is **bit-identical** to `APPINN`.
- **MLP and AP-PEN differ by exactly one scalar** — whether $\alpha^{\mathbb P}$ (hence $\alpha^{\mathbb Q}$, hence the level of $A(\tau)$) is frozen or fitted.

Which is precisely why the 5-seed result comes out as it does: there was never a
mechanism by which `e_sde` could regularise extrapolation.

**In the thesis:** `sec:holdout`, `tab:holdout`.

### 4.5 Limitations
- **No Kalman baseline on this split.** Refit the KF on `<2024` and compute its out-of-sample innovation RMSE. Without it, 1.69× (or 6.71×) has no scale. The KF should do *well* here — it extrapolates via a state equation, not a neural function of $t$.
- $\psi$ is refit on the training window but initialised from a KF fit on the **full** panel — a lookahead leak.
- One split at one date; 2024–26 is a calm, backwardated window. Rolling-origin evaluation would be stronger.
- Aggregated over all 8 maturities; front-month vs long-dated degradation not separated, and roll-down misspecification bites hardest at the front.

## 5. Cross-cutting: the closed-form problem

### 5.1 With $A$ and $B$ analytic, there is no physics-informed loss left
- The GS exponential-affine solution satisfies its own pricing PDE **identically for every $\delta$**. Composing it with a network and differentiating yields a residual that is zero by construction (measured: 4.73e-13 self-consistent, vs a reported 3.68e+02 that was entirely a derivative-consistency artefact).
- AP-PEN takes the honest route: $A$ and $B$ analytic, `e_ode` removed.
- **But the model therefore contains no PDE residual, no ODE residual, no collocation mesh.** What remains is weighted least squares on log-prices, an OU MLE affecting one scalar, and three hinges.
- **Be direct:** AP-PEN is not a PINN in the Raissi sense. It is a **neural-parameterised nonlinear least-squares inversion with an exact analytic forward operator, plus penalties.** "Physics-Enforced" is defensible precisely because the physics is embedded exactly rather than learned approximately — lean on that, don't claim a residual you don't have. (This framing is now in `sec:lit_pinns` and `sec:pinn_inversion`.)

### 5.2 What the network buys — now measured, and the answer is uncomfortable
- Its only job is to represent $\delta_t$ as a smooth function of $t$: 1001 (or 2770) unknowns replaced by ~2200 weights. Not a compression.
- Given exactness and linearity in $\delta$, the per-date optimum is closed-form. **That baseline is now run** (§1.3, §2.2, §4.2):
  - **Synthetic: LS wins decisively** (δ RMSE 0.0087 vs 0.051–0.053 across seeds; innovations 102.8% vs 24–32%).
  - **Real: LS and the networks agree closely**, and all three differ from the KF.
  - **Holdout: LS brackets the neural results**, and the neural extrapolation is worse than simply freezing $\delta$ for two of three variants.
- Candidate things the network could still buy: values *between* observation dates; a differentiable path allowing joint $\psi$/$\delta$ optimisation; and **a place to attach the hinge constraints**, which the per-date solve cannot express.

**That last is the only one with supporting evidence, and §4.3 narrows it
further.** AP-PEN (ARB) is the sole variant that generalises dependably on real
data (0.93×) — but on synthetic data it is the *worst* (2.52× no-skill),
because there the guardrails never bind. So the benefit is not "constraints
stabilise the network"; it is "constraints stabilise the network *when they
bind against the data*". That is a real and defensible claim, and a much
narrower one than the thesis currently makes.

### 5.3 What exactness buys you
- No pricing-model error to disentangle from inversion error.
- Exact gradients through the forward operator; no adjoint solver.
- **The identifiability analysis is available analytically** — most inverse problems cannot offer this. You can *prove* what is recoverable rather than observe it.

### 5.4 Scope
- The method requires an **affine** forward model. It transfers to Schwartz–Smith, GS three-factor, and affine term-structure models generally.
- It does **not** transfer to models without closed forms — exactly where a PINN would be most valuable. The interesting sequel applies this architecture where $A$, $B$ must be *learned*. Then `e_ode` is real physics again.
- **Correction (2026-08-08):** an earlier version of this line offered "CIR convenience yield" as an example of a model requiring a learned $A$, $B$. **That is wrong.** A CIR diffusion $\sigma_2\sqrt{\delta}\,dW_2$ has covariance $\sigma_2^2\delta$, which is *affine* in the state, so by the Duffie–Kan criterion it retains an exponential-affine closed form and reproduces the tautology exactly. Escaping the tautology requires leaving the affine class properly — see §5.5. Do not put the CIR example in the thesis.

### 5.5 The non-affine sequel — declared future work, not attempted

**Status: future work. Not implemented, not run.** Recorded here in enough detail
to be written up as a specified direction rather than a vague gesture, and so
the decision not to attempt it is on the record.

**The minimal escape is CEV on the convenience yield**, $d\delta = \kappa(\alpha^{\mathbb Q}-\delta)dt + \sigma_2(\delta-\delta_{\min})^\gamma dW_2$ with $\gamma \notin \{0,\tfrac12\}$. Covariance $\sigma_2^2(\delta-\delta_{\min})^{2\gamma}$ is non-affine, so no exponential-affine closed form exists and the residual becomes load-bearing.

**Why it is architecturally cheap.** The closed form enters the codebase in exactly one function — `log_F_hat_fn`, at [appen.py:168](../scripts/experiments/appen.py#L168) and [appen.py:212](../scripts/experiments/appen.py#L212). Path net, `e_sde`, all three hinges, balancer, alternating optimiser and data loaders never see anything but `log_F_hat`. Two structural simplifications survive the switch:

- **Spot still factors out.** With $\hat F = S\,G(\tau,\delta)$ the $\hat F_{SS}$ term vanishes and $S$ divides out exactly, leaving a **1+1 dimensional** PDE in $(\tau,\delta)$:
  $$G_\tau = (r-\delta)G + \big[\kappa(\alpha^{\mathbb Q}-\delta) + P\delta^\gamma\big]G_\delta + \tfrac12\sigma_2^2\delta^{2\gamma}G_{\delta\delta}, \quad G(0,\delta)=1$$
- **$r$ factors out too.** Writing $g=\log G = r\tau + \hat g$ cancels $r$ identically, so the network takes $(\tau,\delta)$ only and per-date $r_i$ stays analytic. (Consistency check: in the current $A(\tau)$, $r$ appears only in the $r\tau$ term.)
- The IC can be **hard-constrained** as $\hat g_\theta = \tau\cdot N_\theta(\tau,\delta)$, giving $\hat g(0,\cdot)=0$ by construction — stylistically the same move AP-PEN already makes, so no IC penalty is needed.

**Two results it would deliver that this thesis cannot.**

1. **A verification chain with an exact anchor.** At $\gamma=0$ the CEV model collapses *exactly* to Gibson–Schwartz, so the solution net can be regression-tested against `gibson_schwartz.py` in float64. Then continue to $\gamma\neq0$ and validate against Crank–Nicolson finite differences.
2. **It should restore identifiability of $\alpha^{\mathbb Q}$ — and §0's algebra predicts why.** The $\delta$-drift is $\kappa\alpha^{\mathbb Q} - \kappa\delta + P\delta^\gamma$. At $\gamma=0$ the constants merge into $(\kappa\alpha^{\mathbb Q}+P)$, which *is* the nullity-2 result: only $m=\alpha^{\mathbb Q}+P/\kappa$ is visible. At $\gamma\neq0$, $\delta^\gamma$ and the constant are linearly independent functions of $\delta$, so the two separate. Claim level: **generic local identifiability, testable numerically** with the machinery in [identif.py](../scripts/experiments/identif.py) — not a theorem.

**What it would *not* fix.** $\sigma_1$ and $\rho$ still enter only through $P=\rho\sigma_1\sigma_2$, because the $S$-factorisation kills the $\hat F_{SS}$ term. That half of §0 is a property of futures under *any* multiplicatively separable spot model, not a GS quirk — which is a stronger and more general statement than the thesis currently makes, and worth one sentence in §5.4 regardless.

**Bonus: the floor constraint becomes native.** Under shifted CEV with $\gamma\geq\tfrac12$ and a Feller-type condition, $\delta=\delta_{\min}$ is unattainable by the diffusion. The floor hinge stops being a penalty and becomes a property of the dynamics — the "constraints native, not bolted on" property, obtained without leaving the reduced-form setting.

**Cost, if it is ever picked up.** ~400 new lines (solution net ~40; residual + collocation ~80; CN finite-difference reference ~120; $\gamma=0$ regression test ~40; LS-baseline replacement by FD-solve plus per-date 1-D root find ~60), ~50 changed. Risks: solver-in-the-loop bias (an inaccurate $\hat g_\theta$ can reduce data misfit *by being wrong* — pre-train at fixed $\psi$, and always report PINN-vs-FD solver error beside every $\hat\psi$); $\psi$ moves during training so $\hat g_\theta$ must be re-fine-tuned each outer step (the existing alternating scheme already does this — do **not** make $\psi$ a network input, that is an operator-learning problem); and **the Kalman benchmark breaks** under CEV (state no longer conditionally Gaussian, measurement equation no longer linear) — either keep the affine KF as a deliberately misspecified benchmark or move to a UKF/particle filter.

**Theory-of-storage with inventory (Routledge–Seppi–Spatt 2000) was considered and rejected as infeasible**, despite being the better answer to the motivation. Four compounding costs: (i) RSS is a discrete-time rational-expectations equilibrium solved by dynamic programming, so a continuous-time formulation must be derived before a residual exists at all; (ii) inventory is a *second* latent state whose only proxy (EIA weekly crude stocks) is weekly, revised, and geographically specific rather than the global inventory the theory needs; (iii) complementarity/free-boundary PINN training is materially less reliable than a smooth residual; (iv) the parameters are preferences and technology, not reduced-form, so calibrating them to a futures panel is a separate research programme. **This belongs in Further Work as a well-motivated direction, framed as a PhD-scale extension.**

## 8. What you can and cannot claim

### Can claim
- **The Gibson–Schwartz futures term structure identifies only three combinations of its five risk-neutral parameters** — $(\kappa,\sigma_2,m)$ with $m=\alpha^{\mathbb Q}+\sigma_1\sigma_2\rho/\kappa$ — proven analytically and confirmed to machine precision on both grids. The estimator recovers all three to within 1–4% across 20 draws and, correctly, fails on the other two. **This is the thesis's strongest and most transferable result.**
- A physics-embedded neural inversion recovers the latent path at δ-RMSE 0.051 ± 0.004 against a no-skill baseline of 0.246, robustly across 20 independent noise draws, 5 seeds, and a 10× noise increase.
- **The no-arbitrage hinge mechanism does measurable work on real data**: it drives the cash-and-carry violation rate from a model-free baseline of 1.84% to exactly 0.00%, and cuts implausible convenience-yield states from 7.4% of dates to 0.7% (§2.6). It is also the only mechanism tested that delivers dependable out-of-sample price stability (0.93× ± 0.33 vs 5.7–6.7× unconstrained) — **but only where it binds**: on synthetic data the true δ_t itself lies below the floor on 4.30% of dates, so the constraint penalises states the process genuinely visits, and the same variant becomes the worst of the three (§4.3). Whether the constraint helps is decided by the *observed* violation rate, computable before any model is fitted.
- The $1/\kappa$ pole is a **documented, reproducible failure mode** of the fixed-$\lambda_2$ reparameterisation, identical across all five seeds.

### Cannot claim
- That AP-PEN outperforms the Kalman filter on real data. Two of three variants are stably worse than a constant; the third is parameter-degenerate. **Out of sample the filter beats them outright** (1.07× vs 5.7–6.7×) on matched information.
- That the neural parameterisation beats direct least squares. **On synthetic data it loses by 5.8×**, over five seeds.
- That the transition channel buys out-of-sample robustness. It does not replicate over seeds, and the architecture provides no mechanism for it.
- That fixing $\lambda_2$ resolved the identifiability problem. It converted bias to variance and introduced a pole.
- Any parameter reading of $\sigma_1$, $\rho$ or $\alpha^{\mathbb Q}$.
- **That the recovered path is usable outside its trained time window.** It is worse than a constant there (§4.3). Any framing of AP-PEN as an online or forward-looking estimator is unsupported without an architectural change.

### The honest narrative

> A physics-embedded neural inversion recovers latent convenience yield from
> futures term structures as accurately as the information in those prices
> permits — and the analytic tractability of the Gibson–Schwartz model makes it
> possible to state exactly what that limit is, and to show that the incumbent
> method faces the same limit through the same channel. That same tractability,
> however, removes the PDE residual that defines a PINN and admits a closed-form
> competitor the network does not beat on simulated data. The network
> interpolates the latent state well and does not extrapolate it at all. What
> the neural parameterisation does buy is a differentiable path on which
> economic inequality constraints can act, and where those constraints bind
> against the data they deliver out-of-sample stability nothing else here
> achieves. That — not accuracy, and not generalisation in the usual sense — is
> where it earns its place.

That is a better MSc thesis than the one where everything worked, and it is the
one the evidence supports.
